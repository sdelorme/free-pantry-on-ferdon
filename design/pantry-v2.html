Left: the wall as a neighbour finds it after scanning. Fifteen cards, which is roughly a year at the rate you expect. Cards overlap by a few pixels and sit a degree off square, so it reads as a pile people keep adding to rather than a feed. Shuffle sits opposite the filters.

Right: the post-submit screen you approved — their own words, in the card they'll become, with the "see what others wrote" CTA you asked for. The anonymity promise sits directly under the card, where it can be checked against what's actually on screen.

Prompt stays put, two columns

The hand-lettered prompt is painted on the wall, so it doesn't scroll — pinned left at full height while cards go past it. The blank stays in front of you the whole time you're reading other people's answers to it, which is the thing I wanted to keep from the Chang reference.

Two columns, not four. At dozens of responses a year, four columns would have left most of the screen empty. Two keeps the wall looking tended. It's a one-line change when the board outgrows it.
