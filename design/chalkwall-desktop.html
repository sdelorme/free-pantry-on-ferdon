Desktop · Layout A

Stencil stays put

The prompt is painted on the wall, so it doesn't scroll — it's pinned to the left at full viewport height while cards accumulate past it on the right. Why I'd pick this one: it's the layout that only makes sense for this project. The blank stays in front of you the whole time you're reading other people's answers to it, which is exactly what standing at Chang's wall feels like. Filters sit at the bottom of the panel, always in reach, never competing with the headline.

What it costs: the panel eats 30% of the screen permanently, so cards get three columns instead of four. On a short laptop screen the panel gets cramped.

Desktop · Layout B

Banner wall

Prompt across the top at full width, set very large, then four columns of cards underneath. Why you might pick this one: the headline gets to be enormous, which is the single strongest thing about the Chang reference, and the wall gets the whole screen width — more cards visible at once, better once the board has a few hundred on it.

What it costs: the prompt scrolls away in about one flick, so for most of the time you're on the page the blank isn't there. It's also a layout you've seen before.
